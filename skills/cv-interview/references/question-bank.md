# Question bank

Pick from these; don't ask them all. Ask in numbered batches of 4–7, each answerable in a line, and invite "skip" and "hard to say". Fill the blanks with their real project or role name so the question is concrete.

## The meat of a line

- **Scale:** How many people, customers, users, teams, releases, tickets, markets? How big was the budget?
- **Before and after:** What was it like before you did this, and after? What changed for the people who used it?
- **Time:** How long did it take? How long did the old way take?
- **Your part:** What did you do yourself, and what did the team do? Did you start it, lead it, build it, or join it?
- **What it replaced:** What existed before? Why was it changed?
- **Who noticed:** Did anyone outside your team use it, quote it or ask for it? Was it written up anywhere?
- **What it led to:** Is it still in use? Did it grow into something else?

## When they can't remember

- Is there a deck, a review, a launch post, a dashboard, or a Slack thread about this?
- Did you present it anywhere? Is the recording or the slides still around?
- Did anyone send you a thank-you or a shout-out about it?
- Would a rough range from that document work, or should we leave the number out?

Never offer a number yourself, not even as "about".

## Roles the CV hides

Ask every question in this group, in its own batch. A nearby question doesn't count: "did you work with other teams?" won't surface customer calls.

- Did you lead people? How many, for how long? Did you hire? Let anyone go?
- Did anyone you managed or mentored grow into another role?
- Did you lead through something hard: layoffs, re-orgs, a crisis, a merger, a war, a pandemic?
- Did you step into a gap nobody owned? What was the official title, if any?
- Did you work with other teams regularly? Which ones, and on what?
- Did you talk to customers or users? How often, and what came of it?
- Did you run something on the side: a process, an internal tool, a club, onboarding, hiring loops, a guild?
- Did you teach, write, speak, or organise an event?

## How they use each tool

For each tool or technology on the CV:

- Do you use it hands-on, through an AI coding agent, occasionally, or did your team use it under you?
- Could you answer basic questions about it in an interview? Could you do a live exercise in it?
- What's one thing you built or changed with it?

List it the way they use it ("Next.js, built with AI coding agents"), or move it into a bullet as context.

## Traits, proven

When they have a recommendation, review, or list of traits others see in them:

- For each trait, which line in the CV shows it? (Do this mapping yourself first, then show it.)
- For a trait with no line: Can you give me one example, with what happened and what came of it?

## The defence check

- Could you talk about this line for five minutes in an interview?
- If they asked "how exactly did you do that?", what would you say?
- Is there anything here you'd feel uncomfortable being asked about?
- Is any of this confidential (internal numbers, client names, code names)? Offer the options rather than a yes/no: exact numbers with the source, a fair rounding, the result without numbers, or left out (see SKILL.md step 6).

## Career changers

- Why this move, in one sentence? (This becomes the bridge in the summary.)
- What have you done that the new role also does, even under a different name?
- What have you learned, built or taken on to get ready: a course, a project, volunteer work?

## Reactions to the draft

Often the best questions are about the draft itself:

- Which lines sound like you, and which don't?
- Is anything here not quite true, or overstated?
- Is anything missing that you're proud of?
- Which line would you most want them to ask about?
