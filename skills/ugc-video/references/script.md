# Writing the script

Last checked 2026-10-21.

## Shape

- 20 to 28 seconds spoken, about 60 to 75 words. Short-form ads lose most viewers in the first 3 seconds, so the hook carries the most weight.
- Story order: **hook → the problem, shown on screen → why it happens → what the product does → what the viewer does next (you act, not us) → CTA**.
- Write it as 4 lines of at most about 25 words each. Avatar video models make clips of about 10 seconds, so one line is one clip. The last line is short ("Link in bio. ..."), roughly 3 seconds.
- Every line has a screen behind it. If a line has no matching recording, change the line or record the screen. Never fill with stock footage.

## Hooks

Hook types that show up again and again in UGC ad guides (vendor blogs, not independent studies; test, don't trust):

| Type | Pattern | Example shape |
|---|---|---|
| Challenge | Tell the viewer to do something right now, on their phone | "<Trade>, do this right now. Open <app> and type: <query>." |
| Direct question | Ask the one question the product answers | "<Trade>: when <buyer> asks <app> for <thing>, does it say your name?" |
| Visual + spoken | The avatar shows something while saying it | Holds a phone to camera: "Look who <app> picks for <thing>." |
| Surprise | A true fact the viewer doesn't expect | "<App> recommends <trade> now. And it doesn't read your reviews." |
| Pain point | Name a symptom, point at a check | "If <symptom> this year, check this." (never claim the cause) |

- Generic openers ("Hey guys", "So...", "In today's video") are the clearest sign an ad will underperform.
- Write 3 hooks. Make each as its own first clip; clips 2 to 4 stay the same. Compare 3-second view rate and hold rate.
- Avoid the testimonial hook ("I was struggling with X, then I found Y"). With an AI presenter it is a fake testimonial.

## Honesty rules (hard)

1. **The avatar never claims to use the product or to have results.** It can be a persona in the trade (a caterer, a photographer) giving other people a tip. "I got on three lists" or "my bookings went up" is a fake testimonial.
2. **No invented numbers.** A number on screen comes from a real run of the product, or is labeled "Example". The voice says no numbers unless they are real and on screen.
3. **No made-up anecdotes** ("A client told me..."). Softer than a fake result, still invented.
4. **Ask, don't promise.** "Is it naming you?", not "Get recommended". If the user acts last (sends, posts, applies), the script says so plainly.
5. **Don't claim causes you can't prove.** "If your inquiries got quieter, check this", never "this is why".
6. **Disclose the AI presenter** when posting (see posting.md).

The ethics test for any persuasion move: does it help the viewer decide, or decide for them? Urgency is fine only when it's real ("before your next couple does" is a reason, not a countdown).

## Lines that sound human

- Contractions, short sentences, one thought per sentence.
- Start with a filler when the hook allows it ("Okay, caterers, ..."). It reads as real speech.
- Write numbers and product names the way they should be said. Brand names a voice model might misread get spelled the way they sound in the prompt only, never in captions.
- Read each line aloud and time it: about 2.7 words per second at the delivery you want.

## Slop check before you hand it over

- Could an unrelated product use this line unchanged? Rewrite it toward the real specifics (the trade, the app, the real query).
- No "game-changer", "unlock", "supercharge", "level up", "here's the thing", "It's not X, it's Y".
- No em dashes in anything a person reads.
- The project's own copy rules win over everything here: check for a brand, social or Instagram rules file first.
